About Me

I am a PhD candidate in Computer Science at the University of North Dakota, with an expected graduation date of December 2026. My research spans biomedical natural language processing, large language models, clinical imaging, and multimodal learning for biomedical information extraction and clinical decision support.

In the Hur Lab, I develop transformer- and LLM-based systems for named entity recognition, relation extraction, protein–protein interaction mining, vaccine adjuvant and mechanism extraction, and ontology-grounded evidence structuring. My dissertation develops a multimodal clinical-risk framework that integrates medical imaging with longitudinal clinical variables for TAVR risk prediction.

My work includes first-author research in Bioinformatics Advances, an oral presentation at the AMIA Annual Symposium 2025, and contributions to Vaxjo 2.0 and the Vaccine Ontology. A multilayer BERT system I co-developed placed second in the BioASQ12 BioNNE nested named entity recognition challenge. I also contributed a natural-language-to-PubMed query translation module to the PubMed Miner project during the 2025 NIAID–BRC AI Codeathon at Argonne National Laboratory.
